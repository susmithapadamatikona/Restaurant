How Limited Plates Work

Short runs, honest reasons.

01

Tied to a single delivery

Most specials exist because one grower had a good week. When that crate is gone the dish leaves the menu, usually within four or five services.

02

Tested at the counter first

Every special runs at the chef counter for two nights before it reaches the dining room, so the pacing and portion are already settled.

03

Never repeated identically

A dish that returns next season returns changed. The produce is different, so pretending otherwise would be dishonest.

Allergens & Substitutions

Tell us early and we will rebuild, not remove.

Specials are the hardest dishes to adapt on the night because the produce is finite. With 48 hours of notice the kitchen can plan a parallel plate instead of striking a course from your menu.

The Producers

Three growers behind this season's plates.

Kent, UK

Halbrook Farm

Heritage beetroot, forced rhubarb, and the sorrel that finishes the garden tartare. Family-run since 1958 and still delivering in the same van.

Cornwall, UK

The Wren day boats

Four boats, landed and driven the same morning. If the sea is bad we serve something else rather than buying from a market.

Kerala, India

Malabar pepper co-op

Pepper, cardamom, and vanilla bought direct from a fourteen-family co-operative. Anika visits the harvest every second year.
