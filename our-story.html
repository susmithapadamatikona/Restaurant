Our Story

From a twelve-seat tasting room to a multi-branch culinary house.

  • Founded 2009
  • 12 seats to 7 rooms
  • One shared standard
Since 2009

It started with twelve seats and one borrowed oven.

Every chapter since has been built around craft, restraint, and service that remembers you.

Rafael Moreau signed the lease on Astor Lane with enough money for six months. The room sat twelve, the menu ran to four courses, and the wine list was a single page taped inside a folder. The first year lost money. The second year the counter filled every night, and it has not really emptied since.

Milestones

Seventeen years, told in five rooms.

2009

Astor Lane opens

Twelve seats, four courses, and a kitchen small enough that the pass doubled as the prep bench.

2014

The cellar is dug

We took the basement, lined it with the reserve list, and started seating private dinners underground.

2019

First branch abroad

Marylebone House opens in London with the same menu discipline and a much better cheese cart.

2026

Seven city rooms

Tokyo joins as the smallest room in the group and the only one that serves dinner exclusively.

The Founder

Rafael Moreau on the first year.

"We could not afford a second chef, so I cooked every service for eleven months. It taught me exactly how much a menu can carry before it breaks."

That constraint became the house rule. Nine dishes on the carte, never more. Anything that cannot be executed perfectly by a brigade under pressure does not go on the menu, however good it tastes at 3 PM in an empty kitchen.

Philosophy

What we kept when everything else scaled.

One standard, seven rooms

Each branch adapts to its market and its produce, but the pacing, the training, and the pass discipline are identical.

Promote from inside

Four of our seven head chefs started as commis in the Astor kitchen. The fifth started as a dishwasher in Mumbai.

Waste is a design failure

Trim becomes stock, stock becomes glaze, glaze becomes the next menu. We measure it weekly and publish it internally.

What We Got Wrong

The parts nobody puts in a press release.

2012

The thirty-two dish menu

We expanded the carte to compete with the brasserie next door. Quality dropped, waste tripled, and we spent a year quietly cutting it back to nine.

2016

The Berlin room

Opened on ambition rather than a plan, with no head chef ready to run it. Closed after fourteen months. It remains the most expensive lesson we have bought.

2021

The delivery experiment

Our food travels badly and we should have known. Six weeks in we stopped, refunded the last orders, and went back to cooking for a room.

Then and Now

Seventeen years, side by side.

Almost everything scaled. The two numbers that matter most barely moved, which was the entire point.

In the Press

How the story was told from outside.

2011

"Twelve seats, no compromise."

The first review we ever received, filed by a critic who had to queue on the street for it. We still have it framed by the pass.

2019

"Exporting discipline, not a brand."

On the London opening, and the decision to send our own brigade rather than franchise the name to a local operator.

2025

"The rare group that got smaller."

On cutting the carte across all seven rooms in the same week, three years after we could have coasted instead.
